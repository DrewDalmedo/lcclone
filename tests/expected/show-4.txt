4. Median of Two Sorted Arrays
Difficulty: Hard
Tags: array, binary-search, divide-and-conquer

Two arrays, nums1 and nums2, are each sorted in non-decreasing order. Picture
them merged into a single sorted array, and return the median of that merged
array.

The median of a sorted list is:

  1. its middle element, when the length is odd;
  2. the mean of its two middle elements, when the length is even.

At least one of the two arrays is non-empty.

Example 1:
  Input: nums1 = [1,5,9], nums2 = [2,7]
  Output: 5.0
  Explanation: Merged, the values are [1,2,5,7,9], and the middle one is 5.

Example 2:
  Input: nums1 = [3,8], nums2 = [1,4]
  Output: 3.5
  Explanation: Merged: [1,3,4,8]. The middle pair averages to (3 + 4) / 2 = 3.5.

Constraints

  - 0 <= nums1.length, nums2.length <= 1000
  - 1 <= nums1.length + nums2.length
  - -10^6 <= nums1[i], nums2[i] <= 10^6

| Aim for O(log(m + n)) time, where m and n are the two lengths. A linear merge
| passes too, but misses the point of the problem.
