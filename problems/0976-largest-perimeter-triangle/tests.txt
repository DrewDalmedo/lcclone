# Largest Perimeter Triangle.

@example 9, 6 and 4, the three longest sticks, make a triangle, since 6 + 4 > 9.
[3,6,2,4,9]
=> 19

@example 10, 6 and 4 lie flat (6 + 4 = 10), so the best triangle is 6, 4 and 3.
[10,4,6,3]
=> 13

@example From 5 up, each length is the sum of the two just below it, so any three lie flat or worse.
[5,2,3,13,8]
=> 0

# Three sticks: a triangle, a flat triple, and one stick too long.
[3,4,5]
=> 12

[2,5,3]
=> 0

[1,1,3]
=> 0

# Three sticks of the largest length. (Heron's formula with 64-bit integers overflows here.)
[1000000,1000000,1000000]
=> 3000000

# One long stick can't be used twice: only the three 1s make a triangle.
[1000000,1,1,1]
=> 3

# Two sticks of 10^6 and a stick of 1 still make a (very thin) triangle.
[1,1000000,1,1000000]
=> 2000001

# Only the two 2s and the 3 make a triangle; dropping repeated lengths loses it.
[2,20,3,10,2]
=> 7

# The top three fail, and so do the next ones: the answer uses none of the three longest.
[18,50,4,9,30,7]
=> 20

# Many triangles: the largest uses the three longest sticks, not the first one found
# from the short end.
[3,4,5,6,7]
=> 18

# Two halves of 10^6 lie flat with it; the answer drops to the next longest side.
[500000,1,1000000,500000]
=> 1000001

# The longest list with no triangle: the first 30 Fibonacci numbers, up to 832040.
