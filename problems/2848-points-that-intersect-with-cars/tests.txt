@example [12,15] and [14,20] overlap and together reach from marker 12 to marker 20, which is 9 markers; [3,5] adds markers 3, 4 and 5.

=> 12

@example Marker 7 was logged twice, and both times it lies inside [2,9], which has 8 markers.

@example Marker 40 is the end of one span and the start of the other; it counts once, so markers 30 to 45 give 16.

# After a short span inside a longer one, the next span must be compared
# with the end of the longer one, not with the end of the short one.
[[20,30],[5,40],[8,9]]
=> 36

# A chain of spans that each share an end marker with the next: a merge
# that joins spans only when one starts strictly before the other ends
# counts markers 10 and 20 twice.
[[20,30],[1,10],[10,20]]
=> 30

# Spans side by side, with no gap and nothing shared.
[[5,9],[10,14]]
=> 10

# A gap of one marker (10) between two spans.
[[11,14],[5,9]]
=> 9

# Gaps of many markers, listed out of order: max - min + 1 would give 100.
[[90,95],[1,2],[100,100],[50,50]]
=> 10

# A short span inside a longer one listed before it must not cut the
# merged span short; [61,61] then continues it.
[[10,60],[20,30],[61,61]]
=> 52

# The car listed first starts last, and a span bridges two others.
