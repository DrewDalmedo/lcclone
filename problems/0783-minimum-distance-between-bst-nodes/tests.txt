# Minimum Distance Between BST Nodes. A case is the tree of station positions
# in level order; the result is the distance between the two closest stations.

@example The stations at 150 and 220 are 70 metres apart, the closest two on the line.
[300,150,800,null,220]
=> 70

@example 4990 and 5000 are closest, though the node 1200 lies between them in the tree.
[5000,1200,9000,null,4990,7000]
=> 10

@example Two stations, so there is only one distance to measure.
[42,null,100000]
=> 99958

# Two stations, the second before the first.
[6,5]
=> 1

# Two stations, the second after the first.
[6,null,8]
=> 2

# Two stations at the two ends of the range: the answer is 10^5.
[100000,0]
=> 100000

# The closest pair includes position 0, which a "previous value" of 0 would skip.
[25,0,60,null,2]
=> 2

# A first position of 5 and a larger answer: a "previous value" of -1 would give 6.
[5,null,80,70]
=> 10

# Every parent and child are at least 60 apart; the answer is 10.
[200,100,300,40,190,210,360]
=> 10

# Neighbours along a level are no closer than 6; the answer is 3.
[1000,400,1600,100,997,1003,1900]
=> 3

# A right path with the closest pair at its deep end.
[3,null,90,null,700,null,702]
=> 2

# A left path with the closest pair at its deep end.
[80000,50000,null,20000,null,19998]
=> 2

# The closest pair is the two smallest positions.
[600,8,950,7]
=> 1

# The closest pair is the two largest positions.
[250,60,640,null,null,638]
=> 2

# A zigzag whose closest pair is a grandparent and grandchild.
[2000,30,null,null,1500,45]
=> 15
