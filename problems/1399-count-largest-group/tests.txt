# Count Largest Group.

@example The numbers 2, 11 and 20 all have digit sum 2; every other digit sum from 1 to 9 has two numbers, and 10 (from 19) has one. Only one group has three.
20
=> 1

@example Digit sums 1 to 8 each get a pair (1 and 10, 2 and 11, ..., 8 and 17), while 9 is alone: eight groups of two.
17
=> 8

@example Below 10 every number has a digit sum of its own, so six groups of one tie.
6
=> 6

# The smallest n.
1
=> 1

# Nine groups of one.
9
=> 9

# 10 joins the group of 1 and breaks the nine-way tie.
10
=> 1

# Nine groups of two (19 adds a smaller group): the most groups that ever tie.
19
=> 9

# 30 is counted: its group (3, 12, 21, 30) is the only one of four.
30
=> 1

# Two groups tie (digit sums 10 and 11, 41 numbers each).
450
=> 2

# Three groups tie above 1000 (digit sums 12, 13 and 14, 75 numbers each).
1038
=> 3

# The largest group has 128 numbers, past what a signed char holds.
1616
=> 1

# Two groups tie with 274 numbers, past what an unsigned char holds.
3570
=> 2

# Two groups tie (digit sums 17 and 18) with 632 numbers each.
9170
=> 2

# 9999 starts a group of its own, digit sum 36.
9999
=> 1

# The largest n; 10000 has digit sum 1.
10000
=> 1
