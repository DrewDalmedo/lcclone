# Last Stone Weight. 0 means no stone is left.

@example 10 and 7 leave a stone of 3; the two stones of 4 wear each other away, and the 3 is left.
[10,4,4,7]
=> 3

@example 8 and 5 leave a stone of 3, which then cancels out the other 3.
[8,3,5]
=> 0

@example 13 and 2 leave 11; then 11 and 1 leave 10.
[1,13,2]
=> 10

# A single stone is never ground.
[1000]
=> 1000

# Two stones of the same weight.
[500,500]
=> 0

# Two stones, the lightest and the heaviest possible.
[1,1000]
=> 999

# All the same weight: an even number of stones leaves nothing, an odd
# number leaves one.
[7,7,7,7]
=> 0

[7,7,7,7,7]
=> 7

# What is left of a stone goes back and is ground again: 20 and 3 leave 17,
# 17 and 2 leave 15, 15 and 1 leave 14. Grinding the stones in sorted
# pairs, (20,3) then (2,1), without putting 17 back first, goes wrong.
[20,1,2,3]
=> 14

# The leftover becomes the heaviest (7), and later lands in the middle (2).
[14,6,13,5,15,4]
=> 1

# The leftover lands between the other weights: 9 and 6 leave 3, among 4,
# 3 and 1.
[9,4,1,6,3]
=> 1

# The leftover is the new lightest: 10 and 9 leave 1, below 8 and 3.
[10,9,8,3]
=> 4

# The heaviest stone listed last: 9 and 6 leave 3, then 4 and 3 leave 1.
# A heap that takes the children of i as 2i and 2i+1 on a 0-based array
# never compares the root with the 9.
[4,6,9]
=> 1

# Three stones tie for the heaviest.
[5,5,5,2]
=> 3

# The pile empties on the very last step: 9 and 6 leave 3, which cancels
# out the other 3.
[3,9,6]
=> 0

# Thirty stones of different weights, in no order.
