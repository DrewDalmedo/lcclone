# Find the Maximum Divisibility Score. The answer is a value taken from
# divisors.

@example 3 divides 6, 15 and 9, for a score of 3; 5 and 2 score 2 each.
[6,10,15,9]
[5,3,2]
=> 3

@example 7 and 2 both score 2; 2 is the smaller, although 7 comes first.
[14,21,8]
[7,4,2]
=> 2

@example No divisor goes into 3 or 5, so all three tie at 0 and the smallest, 6, is the answer.
[3,5]
[11,6,8]
=> 6

# The smallest case.
[1]
[1]
=> 1

# 1 divides every number, so it reaches the highest score possible.
[7,9,25]
[5,1,3]
=> 1

# A divisor equal to a number scores it; one larger than every number
# scores nothing.
[12,4]
[12,50]
=> 12

# Repeats in nums count once each: 9 scores 4, 2 scores 3.
[9,9,9,9,10,14,22]
[2,9]
=> 9

# Repeats in divisors change nothing: 10 and 5 both score 2.
[10,20,7]
[10,10,5,10]
=> 5

# All four tie; the smallest sits in the middle of divisors.
[30,60,7]
[15,3,10,5]
=> 3

# 8, 4 and 2 tie at 3; the smallest is last.
[8,16,24]
[8,4,6,2]
=> 2

# Every score is 0, and divisors[0] is not the smallest.
[2,3,5]
[9,7,4]
=> 4

# A number must be a multiple of the divisor, not the other way round:
# 6 and 4 are multiples of 2 and 3, but neither divides them.
[2,3]
[6,4]
=> 4

# Values at the upper bound: each divisor scores 1, since 10^9 is not a
# multiple of 333333333 but 999999999 is.
[1000000000,999999999]
[1000000000,999999999,333333333]
=> 333333333
