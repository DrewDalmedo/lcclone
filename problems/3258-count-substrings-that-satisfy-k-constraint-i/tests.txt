# Count Substrings That Satisfy K-Constraint I.

@example Only the whole string holds two of each character; the other 9 substrings have at most one "0" or at most one "1".
"0110"
1
=> 9

@example Of the 21 substrings, 5 have two or more of each character: "0011", "0110", "00110", "01101" and the whole string.
"001101"
1
=> 16

@example Only "1111000" and "111000" have three or more of each character. "1111" has four ones but no zeros, so it counts.
"1111000"
2
=> 26

# A single character.
"0"
1
=> 1

"1"
1
=> 1

# k equal to the length: every substring counts.
"0101"
4
=> 10

# "At most k": the whole string has exactly k of each, and counts.
"0011"
2
=> 10

# One small count is enough: 4 substrings have two or more of each
# character, and the other 17 count, though only 7 have at most one of both.
"000111"
1
=> 17

# 50 equal characters: all 1275 substrings count, each by its position
# (only 50 different strings among them), more than a byte-sized count holds.
"11111111111111111111111111111111111111111111111111"
1
=> 1275

# Alternating, k = 1: only the substrings of length 3 or less count.
"01010101010101010101010101010101010101010101010101"
1
=> 147

# Alternating, k = 2: lengths up to 5.
"01010101010101010101010101010101010101010101010101"
2
=> 240

# Alternating, k = 24: every substring but the whole string, which has 25 of
# each.
"01010101010101010101010101010101010101010101010101"
24
=> 1274

# Alternating, k = 25: now the whole string counts too.
"01010101010101010101010101010101010101010101010101"
25
=> 1275

# k at half the length: no substring can have more than k of both.
"010110"
3
=> 21

# Long runs of ones around two zeros: the 16 substrings holding both zeros
# fail, the rest have at most one zero.
"11101110111"
1
=> 50

# Pairs of each character, k = 2: 12 substrings have three or more of each.
"1100110011"
2
=> 43
