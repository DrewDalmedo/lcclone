# Minimum Distance Between Three Equal Elements I. -1 means no value appears three times.

@example The 4s at indices 0, 2 and 4 are 2, 2 and 4 apart, a spread of 8; the 2s at 1, 5 and 6 give 4 + 1 + 5 = 10.
[4,2,4,3,4,2,2]
=> 8

@example 3 and 1 appear twice each and 2 once, so there is no trio.
[3,1,3,1,2]
=> -1

@example 5 appears four times; its copies at indices 3, 4 and 5 have a spread of 1 + 1 + 2 = 4.
[5,3,6,5,5,5]
=> 4

# A single element.
[1]
=> -1

# Two equal elements are not enough.
[2,2]
=> -1

# Three equal neighbours: the smallest spread there is.
[3,3,3]
=> 4

# Every value appears exactly twice.
[4,1,4,6,1,6]
=> -1

# All values different.
[6,2,5,1,3,4]
=> -1

# The only trio uses the first and last positions: 2 * (n - 1).
[7,1,2,7,3,4,7]
=> 12

# 3 has the closest pair, but its third copy is far away; the 5s win.
[3,3,7,5,1,2,5,4,6,5,8,3]
=> 12

# 8 appears four times but loses to three 4s packed together at the end.
[8,3,8,1,8,2,8,4,4,4]
=> 4

# The best trio is at the very start; 1 and 2 interleave after it.
[9,9,9,1,2,1,2,1,2]
=> 4

# The tightest three copies of 2 are neither its first three nor its last three.
[2,1,3,1,2,2,3,2,4,3,5,2]
=> 6

# The value equals the length of nums.
[4,4,1,4]
=> 6
