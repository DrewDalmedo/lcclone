# Sum of Unique Elements.

@example 8 appears twice and 3 three times, so all their copies are struck out; 5 + 2 = 7 is left.
[8,3,8,5,2,3,3]
=> 7

@example 6 appears twice and 2 three times, so every entry is struck out and nothing is left.
[6,2,6,2,2]
=> 0

@example No value repeats, so nothing is struck out: 9 + 1 + 6 = 16.
[9,1,6]
=> 16

# A single entry.
[1]
=> 1

# Both bounds; 100 needs a table slot of its own.
[100,1]
=> 101

# Two equal entries.
[37,37]
=> 0

# One value, four times.
[4,4,4,4]
=> 0

# A value three times: adding it at its first copy and taking it away at
# its second would add it again at its third.
[5,12,5,5]
=> 12

# 6 three times and 2 seven times leave only 9 and 40.
[6,2,6,9,6,2,2,40,2,2,2,2]
=> 49

# The copies of 57 are at the very first and last positions.
[57,12,33,12,80,57]
=> 113

# Once sorted, the repeated values sit at both ends.
[100,1,50,1,100]
=> 50

# A sum of 256, which doesn't fit in a byte.
[100,99,57,3,3]
=> 256
