# Minimum Cost of Buying Candies With Discount.

@example Bundle 10, 8 and 4 (4 free), and pay for 3 alone: 10 + 8 + 3 = 21.
[4,8,3,10]
=> 21

@example One bundle of three; the free candy may cost as much as the paid ones.
[7,7,7]
=> 14

@example Bundles 12, 9, 6 and 5, 4, 3 make 6 and 3 free, and 2 is paid alone.
[12,2,9,4,6,5,3]
=> 32

# A single candy.
[100]
=> 100

# Two candies: nothing is free.
[1,100]
=> 101

# Three different prices: the cheapest is free.
[20,50,35]
=> 85

# Four different prices (n % 3 == 1): freeing 22, the third dearest, beats freeing 15.
[15,40,22,31]
=> 86

# Five different prices (n % 3 == 2): bundle 9, 8, 6 and pay for 2 and 1.
[6,1,9,2,8]
=> 20

# Six different prices: 6 and 1 are free.
[9,1,6,2,5,8]
=> 24

# Six equal prices: two of them are free.
[4,4,4,4,4,4]
=> 16

# The free candy ties the cheaper paid price.
[6,6,3,3,3]
=> 18

# Already in descending order (n % 3 == 1): 50 and 5 are free.
[90,70,50,30,10,5,1]
=> 201

# Ascending order, eight candies (n % 3 == 2): 21 and 5 are free.
[2,3,5,8,13,21,34,55]
=> 115

# Two prices, 1 and 100, mixed: one 100 and one 1 are free.
[1,100,1,100,1,100,100]
=> 302
