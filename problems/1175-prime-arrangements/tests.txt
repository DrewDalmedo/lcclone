# Prime Arrangements. Results are the number of valid layouts modulo
# 10^9 + 7.

@example Cards 2 and 3 must take slots 2 and 3, in either order, which leaves slots 1 and 4 to cards 1 and 4, also in either order: 2 × 2 = 4 layouts.
4
=> 4

@example The primes up to 7 are 2, 3, 5 and 7. Their cards fill the four prime slots in 4! = 24 ways, and cards 1, 4 and 6 fill slots 1, 4 and 6 in 3! = 6 ways: 24 × 6 = 144.
7
=> 144

@example There are 8 primes up to 20, so 8! × 12! = 19313344512000 layouts are valid, which leaves 344376809 after dividing by 10^9 + 7.
20
=> 344376809
