# Buy Two Chocolates.

@example The cheapest pair is 4 and 5, which costs 9 and leaves 3.
[6,4,9,5]
12
=> 3

@example Even the two cheapest bars cost 15, more than 14, so nothing is bought.
[7,8,10]
14
=> 14

@example The two bars priced 2 cost exactly 4, and spending all of it is allowed.
[5,2,5,2]
4
=> 0

# Two bars that cost 2 together, and only 1 to spend.
[1,1]
1
=> 1

# Two bars only: both are bought, and the change is returned, not the cost.
[30,45]
100
=> 25

# The cheapest pair misses the budget by exactly 1.
[50,51]
100
=> 100

# The cheapest bar twice would fit, but it can be bought only once, and
# with the next cheapest it costs 11.
[3,9,8]
10
=> 10

# The cheapest price is on two bars, and both are bought.
[4,7,4,9]
10
=> 2

# Prices falling from first to last: the two cheapest come at the end.
[9,8,7,6,5,4]
10
=> 1

# Several pairs fit: the cheapest is 2 and 3, not the dearest pair that
# fits (9 and 3) or the first one found (2 and 9).
[2,9,3,6,4]
12
=> 7

# Every bar is dear: the cheapest two cost 197.
[99,100,98,100]
100
=> 100

# One bar at 1 and one at the top price, 100: together 101, one more than
# money. A tracker that starts below 100 never records the 100.
[100,1]
100
=> 100

# The cheapest two are the first and the last bar.
[3,10,20,10,4]
