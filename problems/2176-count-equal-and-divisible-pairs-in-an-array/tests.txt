# Count Equal and Divisible Pairs in an Array.

@example Second 0 pairs with each later 6 (three pairs) and the 2s at seconds 1 and 4 give 1 * 4 = 4; the other pairs of 6s give 6, 10 and 15.
[6,2,6,6,2,6]
4
=> 4

@example The 7s sit at seconds 2 and 3, whose product 6 is a multiple of 6 though neither second is. The 4s at seconds 1 and 4 give 4, which is not.
[9,4,7,7,4]
6
=> 1

@example The two 30s sit at seconds 1 and 2, and 1 * 2 = 2 is not a multiple of 4. 30 * 30 is one, but the readings play no part in that test.
[12,30,30,1]
4
=> 0

# A single reading has nothing to pair with.
[42]
7
=> 0

# Two equal readings at the bounds: second 0 makes any k work.
[100,100]
100
=> 1

# The only matching pair is second 0 with the last second (counting
# seconds from 1 would test 1 * 4 instead).
[7,1,2,7]
5
=> 1

# k = 10: 4 * 5 = 20, though neither 4 nor 5 is a multiple of 10.
[1,2,3,4,7,7]
10
=> 1

# k = 9: 3 * 6 = 18, though neither 3 nor 6 is a multiple of 9.
[2,5,1,8,4,3,8]
9
=> 1

# k = 24: the 7s at seconds 3, 4 and 8 match only as (3, 8), product 24;
# (4, 8) gives 32, larger than 24 but not a multiple of it.
[5,1,2,7,7,3,4,6,7]
24
=> 1

# The 3s match on every pair of seconds (0, 2, 4) though 3 * 3 is odd;
# the 8s (seconds 1 and 3) don't, though 8 * 8 is even.
[3,8,3,8,3]
2
=> 3
