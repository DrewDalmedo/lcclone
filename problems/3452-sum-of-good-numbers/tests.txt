# Sum of Good Numbers. An element's rivals are the elements exactly k
# places away on either side.

@example 4 beats its rival 2, and 5 beats its rival 2; the two 9s are each other's rival and tie; 2 loses to both 4 and 5.
[4,9,2,9,5]
2
=> 9

@example With k = 1 the rivals are the neighbours: only 6 beats them all, since each 8 ties with the other.
[3,8,8,1,6]
1
=> 6

@example Each element has one rival: 7 beats 6, and 5 beats 2.
[7,2,6,5]
2
=> 12

# The shortest array: the larger element is good.
[5,9]
1
=> 9

# Two equal elements: neither is good.
[4,4]
1
=> 0

# All equal.
[6,6,6,6,6]
2
=> 0

# Increasing: only the last k elements are good.
[1,2,3,4,5,6,7]
3
=> 18

# Decreasing: only the first k elements are good.
[9,7,5,3,1]
2
=> 16

# k is half the length: each pair of rivals gives its larger value, and
# the tied pair gives nothing.
[3,8,8,2,5,8]
3
=> 11

# The middle element has two rivals and loses to one of them.
[1,9,6,4,7]
2
=> 16

# Comparing with the neighbours instead would give 5 + 6 + 7 = 18.
[1,5,2,6,3,7]
2
=> 10

# A tie with the right rival only, then a tie with the left rival only.
[1,4,4,2,8,5]
1
=> 8

# Equal good values at different indices are both added.
[8,1,8,1]
1
=> 16

# Indices k - 1, k, n - k - 1 and n - k, where a rival is just inside or
# just outside the array: the good elements are 8, 9 and 8.
[8,3,9,7,6,4,5,8]
3
=> 25
