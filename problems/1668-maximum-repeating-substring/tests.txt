# Maximum Repeating Substring.

@example "tic" occurs at 0, 6 and 9. The copies at 6 and 9 touch, giving "tictic"; "tictictic" occurs nowhere.
"tictactictictac"
"tic"
=> 2

@example "aba" occurs at 0, 2, 5 and 8. The copies at 2, 5 and 8 form "abaabaaba"; the copy at 0 shares a letter with the one at 2, so it can't join them.
"ababaabaaba"
"aba"
=> 3

@example All of the letters of "nab" are there, but never in that order.
"banana"
"nab"
=> 0

# The smallest inputs: word is all of sequence.
"a"
"a"
=> 1

# word is longer than sequence.
"ab"
"abc"
=> 0

# sequence is word written 4 times: the chain runs from the first letter to the last.
"xyxyxyxy"
"xy"
=> 4

# Occurrences that overlap don't chain: "aa" occurs 4 times, but only 2 copies fit in a row.
"aaaaa"
"aa"
=> 2

# The largest answer.
"zzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzzz"
"z"
=> 100

# "abcab" ends with its first two letters. The best chain begins at 3, inside the copy at 0; a scan that jumps past that copy finds 1.
"abcabcababcab"
"abcab"
=> 2

# The best chain begins at 5, inside the second copy of the chain at 0 and 3.
"abaababaabaaba"
"aba"
=> 3

# "aabaa" ends with its first two letters: the chain of 3 begins at 3, inside the copy at 0.
"aabaabaaaabaaaabaa"
"aabaa"
=> 3

# Five occurrences in all, but no more than 2 in one chain.
"cdcdxcdcdxcd"
"cd"
=> 2

# Chains of 1, 2 and 3: the longest is the last.
"qwxqwqwxqwqwqw"
"qw"
=> 3

# Two chains of 2, each followed by a copy that falls short: one has a wrong letter, the other is cut off by the end.
"pqrpqrpqspqrpqrpq"
"pqr"
=> 2

# A one-letter word: the answer is the longest run of that letter.
"bbabbbabb"
"b"
=> 3
