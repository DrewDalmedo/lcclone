# Complement of Base 10 Integer. Numbers are written in decimal; the
# explanations spell out their binary forms.

@example 12 is 1100 in binary. Swapping the digits gives 0011, which is 3.
12
=> 3

@example 0 is written as the single digit 0, which becomes 1.
0
=> 1

@example 1000 is 1111101000 in binary; swapped, that is 0000010111, which is 23.
1000
=> 23

# A single 1 becomes 0.
1
=> 0

# 10 becomes 01.
2
=> 1

# 1110 becomes 0001, and 10001 becomes 01110: swapping one digit too many or
# too few gives a different answer.
14
=> 1

17
=> 14

# Just below, at and just above a power of two: 1111111111, 10000000000
# and 10000000001.
1023
=> 0

1024
=> 1023

1025
=> 1022

# 2^29, the largest power of two in range, has the largest complement,
# 2^29 - 1.
536870912
=> 536870911

# 2^29 - 1 is 29 ones, so nothing is left after the swap.
536870911
=> 0

# 2^29 + 1: 30 digits with a 1 at each end.
536870913
=> 536870910

# The largest n has 30 binary digits, so its complement is
# 2^30 - 1 - 999999999.
999999999
=> 73741824

# 0x24924924 is 100 repeated ten times (30 digits); its complement is 011
# repeated ten times, 0x1B6DB6DB.
613566756
=> 460175067
